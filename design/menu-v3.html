The question that moving the frame opens

If the frame moves to the project and the project has two sessions, the menu says which branch you are looking at something in but not which leaf. In all four proposals the block carries the menu's only blue border and the session is marked without a frame: with a fill, with weight or with a bar. What changes is how much.

And there is a second case worth looking at here instead of discovering it in the code: opening a project's page is not the same as opening one of its sessions. Both light up the block. What separates them is what lights up inside — the header or the row. The third project in each mockup is that case.

What I would implement

  1. One blue border in the whole menu, and the project block carries it. That was N3's complaint, and it is fixed by taking the frame off the session, not by adding contrast.
  2. The frame names the branch; the fill names the leaf. Inside the lit block, the session you are looking at is filled in solid blue, in white text. No border, no bar: in a 280 px block, a fill is already unmistakable.
  3. Opening the project page lights up the header, not a session. Same lit block, different row inside. That way the two things called "active" today stop looking alike.
  4. Amber survives intact. A session waiting on you keeps its dot and its badge inside the block, whether the block is lit or not: blue says where you are, amber says where you are needed, and they still do not compete.
  5. Without the per-row frame, the list breathes. The block already groups, so the session needs no border of its own — and the menu goes from eight boxes with sub-boxes to eight boxes with rows.
